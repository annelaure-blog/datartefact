Dead reckoning as a data practice

Dead reckoning estimates position from a known point using speed, direction, and time. A model for historical data work: no ground truth, just accumulated inference — and an honest accounting of the error rate.

Dead reckoning: estimating your current position based on a known past position, plus elapsed time, speed, and heading.

No external reference. No ground truth. Just accumulated inference from what you already know.

This is also what we do with most historical data. We have a starting point, a rough sense of direction, and a lot of accumulated error. The question is not whether the estimate is perfect — it never is — but whether it is good enough to act on.

Knowing the error rate is part of the navigation.
