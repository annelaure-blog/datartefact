Every census is a claim about who belongs and who does not.

## The act of counting

To count a population is to define it first. Before any enumerator knocks on a door, someone has already decided which categories matter — race, occupation, marital status, language — and which do not. Those decisions are never neutral.

## Historical examples

The Roman census was explicitly tied to taxation and military conscription. The British colonial census in India hardened fluid caste identities into fixed administrative categories. The US census has redefined its racial categories in almost every decade since 1790.

In each case, the act of counting shaped the thing being counted.

## What this means for data work

When we inherit a dataset, we inherit the classification system embedded in it. Understanding who built that system, when, and for what purpose is not optional context — it is the analysis.
